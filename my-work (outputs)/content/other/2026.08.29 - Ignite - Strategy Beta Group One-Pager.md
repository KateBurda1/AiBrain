## The full picture on each pain point

**1. The corporate-to-property translation gap.**
The numbers are sophisticated by the time they reach the boardroom and improvised by the time they reach the property. Plans get handed down rather than built up. The people closest to the guest are working from a document they had no hand in building, so they execute it the way you execute somebody else's plan.

*Ignite:* strategy is built with the ground layer, not issued to it. Property teams get a ranked list of what to do next with the reasoning attached.

**2. Signal to action takes a quarter.**
Someone spots the shift early. Almost everyone does. Then it has to be modeled, argued, planned, socialized, and rolled out. By the time the team moves, the window has moved too. That delay is not a strategy failure. It is an operationalization problem.

*Ignite:* continuous detection of the shift (we call it a "quake"), automatic roadmap generation, and the sales and marketing playbook to act on it. Signal to action compresses from months toward weeks.

**3. Two halves of the truth, no bridge.**
You own revenue-projection BI. You own sales and CRM BI. Nothing joins them, so an analyst joins them by hand and the answer arrives after the decision.

*Ignite:* sits on top of both and brings market and competitive landscape into the model, so you are forecasting against the market rather than against last year.

**4. Three plans, one P&L.**
Sales, marketing, and revenue each optimize separately. Nobody is wrong on purpose. Alignment is the mechanism here, not the benefit. The recovered revenue is the benefit.

*Ignite:* one Ignition Score ranking segments, accounts, and actions, shared across all three functions.

**5. The confident story nobody can check.**
Your teams can show completion rates and pipeline hygiene. Ask why last quarter beat plan and you get a confident story, told by people with every incentive to believe it, and no way to test it. Asset managers hear that story on repeat.

*Ignite:* tracks which actions show up in revenue across the portfolio.

## The 16:1 case, in full

A hotel in a major leisure market reported a 16:1 return on ad spend. By any normal reading of that number it was an excellent quarter, and it was reported, celebrated, and repeated upward as one.

The campaign was aimed at the hotel's least valuable customer segment. Once you account for what that segment costs to acquire and what it contributes in gross profit, the threshold that campaign had to clear was 22:1. It cleared 16. The team was six points underwater and reporting a win.

Move the same dollars to a more valuable customer and the required threshold drops to 8:1. **A lower ROAS is the better outcome.** That sentence breaks a rule nobody in marketing questions, which is exactly why the error survives everywhere it exists.

Three things are worth noting about this failure:

- **Nothing was broken.** The tracking was right, the math was right, the report was right. The number was measured against the wrong customer, so a good result and a bad decision produced identical reporting.
- **The incentives protect it.** A team holding a 16:1 has no reason to go looking for the reason it might be a loss. Neither does the person they report it to.
- **This is what "most valuable" has to mean.** Revenue and profitability together. The Ignition Score is Revenue Growth × Gross Profit Margin for exactly this reason. Without that ranking, every downstream metric gets graded against the wrong bar.

What Ignite does with it: first, transparency on real performance against the right threshold. Second, the critical thinking to move that campaign to a more valuable customer, where a lower return wins.

This is a real case Ignite caught, not an illustration. It is not a realized-lift result, and it does not claim to be one. It belongs on the proven side of the ledger for one reason: it shows Ignite sees what the existing reporting stack cannot.

## On the attribution claim, precisely

Ignite does not tell you that one task at one hotel produced $40,000. Nobody can honestly tell you that, and anyone who says otherwise is selling a coincidence with a decimal point.

What Ignite does is watch the same move play out across many assets and many markets, and separate the moves that travel from the moves that were just a good market. The pattern is visible across a portfolio even when a single property's result is not. Then what the best are doing becomes a playbook the others can run.

That is the difference between a system and a story. As James Clear put it, you do not rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your system.
