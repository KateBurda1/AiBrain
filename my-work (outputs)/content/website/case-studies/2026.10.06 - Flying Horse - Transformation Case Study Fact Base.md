## 1. Before state: how it worked when we arrived (Feb to Apr 2025)

Primary source: `CF/Deliverables/Insight Intelligence Report v3.docx` / `.pdf` (dated April 4, 2025). Secondary: `CF/Deliverables/KB Co_FH 8 Cylinder Conversation.pptx` (file dated 2025.04.23, prepared for Jeff Smith and Marlene Green).

### Revenue strategy
- Strategy existed only at the meta-segment level (group vs. transient). "It doesn't drill down to the segment or micro-segment level," so teams could not tell who to target or how to prioritize time. (Insight Intelligence Report, Science & Strategy findings)
- "The overall strategy to achieve numbers was not clear." Teams worked day-to-day on answering leads and inquiries. (same)
- Business acceptance rules (when to take business, space constraints, future dates) "seemed instinctual"; teams relied on "the unwritten rules." (same)
- Leadership view of the before state, from our own 45-day check-in deck: "Budget felt out of reach. How to get there was fuzzy. The team didn't know how to answer our questions." (8 Cylinder Conversation, slide 3)

### Sales
- Sales ran on lead response. "Most conversations are about rates, dates, and space." Very transactional. (Insight Intelligence Report, Process & Customer Engagement)
- The "Talking Brochure": "A client called it the 'Talking Brochure' and it stuck." Salespeople told customers about the hotel instead of creating value for them. (same)
- "Little action was focused on proactive selling." (8 Cylinder, slide 10)
- No account development plans in play. (Insight Intelligence Report)
- Territories were not defined. Market responsibility came from community relationships; group leads were sorted by size, not territory or vertical. (same)
- Team talent read: "high will and medium skill but very high potential." The skill gap was proactive selling. (same, Talent section)

### Marketing
- Outreach was "us first": "In looking through Flying Horse's existing outreach, the customer is rarely mentioned." (Insight Intelligence Report)
- Marketing operated as a shared resource with each team working "somewhat independently." (same)
- Prospecting relied on backward-looking data: they paid for Knowland (`CF/Initial Pitch and Agreement/Flying Horse Resort & Club_Agreement.pdf`, 2023 order; later costed at $17,000/yr in `CF/1- Science & Strategy/Sales Tech Stack FH.pptx`, slide 3).

### Measurement
- Focus was On the Books, Pick-up, and Booked Revenue. "The gap lies in funnel / pipeline management and the measurement prior to the 'On-The-Books, Pick Up, or Contract.'" (Insight Intelligence Report, Performance & Metrics)
- "Currently we have budget goals, which is an outcome or result of effort." No measurement of effort against strategy. (same)
- No pipeline report existed; it was recommended as the first step to a dashboard. (same)

### Team and structure
- "Although teams work closely together, they are not connected and are siloed." (Insight Intelligence Report, Structure)
- "There was a lack of a commercial leader." (same)
- Redundancy: two administration positions, three people involved in systems administration. (same)
- About a day and a half per week spent on data entry between systems. (same) The 8 Cylinder deck states "Teams using 1 ½ days a week for data input" and a contact database of "18K" not being worked: "Capital is in our bank account, but it isn't working for us." (slide 12)
- "Workflow between players wasn't clear flow or hand off, but rather a 'catch.'" Job descriptions were "job by assignment." (Insight Intelligence Report)
- Structure diagnosis: "The flat, loosely organized structure at Flying Horse leads to segmented silos, role confusion, and inefficient workflows." (8 Cylinder, slide 14)

- Transient occupancy ranked 6 of 6 in the comp set in Jan and Feb 2026 (`CF/Monthly STAR_66624-20260800-USD-E.xlsx`, Segmentation Ranking tab).

- Flying Horse earns $978.67 total revenue per occupied room vs. a comp set average of $345.59. (same)
