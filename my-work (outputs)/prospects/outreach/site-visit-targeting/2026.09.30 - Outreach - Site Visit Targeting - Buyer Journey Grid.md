# Site Visit Targeting: Buyer Journey Grid

**Draft for Kate's review, 2026.09.30**

Stage 1 confirmed. Stages 2 to 5 are retired by the router decision below; kept for reference only.

Built with the intent-series construct. Each stage runs in order: what changed, thinking and feeling, focus, what we have, impact, value proposition, message.


**Decided by Kate 2026.09.30: Site Visit Targeting is a router, one touch only.** Touch 1 shows "what we've been working on" as a designed email with one box per finished series. The box they click enrolls them in that series' cadence: Climate to Business Transformation, Right or Lucky to Sales Intelligence, ABM to ABM. No Site Visit Targeting Touches 2 to 5 get built. As more series are built through the construct, they get added as boxes. Later: target the specific pages each company looked at.

## Who this is for

- **56 people at 25 companies.** Each company had a real visit to kateburda.com: 2+ pages and 30+ seconds in the last 3 months.
- **Who they are:** tagged "SVT Approved" in Zoho. They're senior commercial leaders: CROs, CMOs, CSOs, CCOs, VPs of Sales and Marketing. No CEOs yet. The old ZoomInfo settings never bought them, and the new settings will.
- **What we don't know:** why they came. The person we write to may not be the person who visited.
- **The rule that follows:** we never say "you visited our site."
- **Account-based:** there are 2 or 3 people per company, and we treat each company as one conversation.

## How the series works

- **Stage 1 gives them a choice of three papers.** Their click is logged in Zoho and tells us what they care about.
- **Stage 2 follows the thread they picked.** People who don't click get their own version.
- **Stage 3 brings all three threads together.** Climate, luck and ABM are three doors into the same room: the plan isn't built on where next year's growth really comes from, and sales, marketing and finance aren't working from one version of it.
- **Stages 4 and 5 are the same for everyone.**
- **Five stages, five touches.** Touch 1 carries Stage 1, through Touch 5 and Stage 5.

## Stage 1: Point of inspiration (confirmed)

**What changed:** someone at their company spent real time on kateburda.com. They haven't asked for anything and don't know we noticed.

**Thinking and feeling (the addressee, a senior commercial leader):**
- "Next year's plan is due and I'm on the hook for the number. Where's the growth coming from?"
- "Are we putting the team and the budget on the right customers, or just the familiar ones?"
- "Everyone's pitching me AI and data. What's worth my time?"
- Feeling: stretched and guarded. They'll open anything that reads like insight, not a pitch.
- The committee: the CEO wants a plan they can defend. The CFO wants the gap in dollars. Marketing is defending spend it can't tie to revenue. Sales wants better accounts, not more of them.

**Focus:**
- Getting next year's plan and number right before the budget locks.
- Money and people where the return is.
- Their time. Only what's clearly useful.
- No pressure. They aren't shopping.

**What we have:** three papers, 28 years of watching plans get built on last year plus a percentage, and Zoho click tracking.

**Impact:** real work, no strings. We learn their interest from their click instead of guessing.

**Value proposition:** because you're building next year's plan and don't have time for another pitch, we hand you the work itself, three short papers on what we're seeing this planning season, so you take what's useful and skip the rest.

**Message (confirmed, option A):**

- Subject: Three things we've been working on
- Body: It's planning season, and three questions keep coming up with the leaders we work with. Is next year's plan built for the market we're in? Were last year's best results right, or just lucky? And are sales and marketing working the same accounts, or just the same logos? We wrote a short paper on each. Take whichever is useful. No call, no follow-up pitch.

**Logging:** each paper gets its own tracked link. A click tags the contact "SVT Chose Climate", "SVT Chose Lucky" or "SVT Chose ABM".

## Stage 2: Informed (draft, branches by choice)

**What changed:** they opened one paper, and its question is now in their head. Or they didn't click, and the email is a name they half remember.

**Thinking and feeling, by thread:**

- **Climate:** "Our plan assumes the market we had last year." "Which of our bets were built for a different climate?" Feeling: a small itch. The paper named something they'd been stepping around.
- **Lucky:** "Pick our best result from last year. Would I bet on it again?" "Would my forecast hold up if someone asked why?" Feeling: uneasy. They know some of last year's wins were timing.
- **ABM:** "Marketing and sales both say they're on our top accounts. Why doesn't it feel coordinated?" "Are we on the same stage with the same account, or two plans aimed at the same logo?" Feeling: irritated, because they've funded ABM and can't see it working as one motion.
- **No click:** too busy, or not the right moment. Nothing against us yet.
- **The committee, all threads:** the CEO is asking for the plan. The CFO wants the gap in dollars before approving anything. The other commercial chair (CMO to the CRO, or the reverse) has its own version of the same doubt.

**Focus:**
- One clear question they can take into the planning room.
- Not more reading. Something they can use this week.
- Looking sharp in front of the CEO and CFO when the plan gets challenged.

**What we have, by thread:**
- **Climate:** the report's questions, Outside-In Thinking (plan from the customer's customer, not last year's spreadsheet).
- **Lucky:** the 10 questions, the Ignition Score (revenue and profitability together), the 16:1 story.
- **ABM:** the Revenue Trifecta (which accounts, what message, how it ties back to revenue) and the six-stage ABM Engine.
- **No click:** a one-line version of each paper.

**Impact:** they walk into planning with one sharp test instead of a hunch, and they remember where it came from.

**Value proposition:** because the paper's question is now on your desk, we give you the one test to run before the plan locks, so you bring an answer to the planning meeting instead of a hunch.

**Messages, by thread (recommended first):**
- **Climate:** "Is your plan built for the market you had, or the one you're in?" Alternate: "Most plans are forecasts of last year with better formatting."
- **Lucky:** "Pick your best result from last year. Would you bet on it again?" Payoff line: "Be right, not just lucky." Alternate: "Your forecast has a lot of confidence for something built on last year."
- **ABM:** "Same account. Same week. Same message?" Alternate: "Two plans, one logo. That isn't account-based. That's a coincidence."
- **No click:** "In case the timing was off." Three one-line summaries. Reply 1, 2 or 3 and we'll send the one you want.

## Stage 3: Explore (draft, all threads come together)

**What changed:** they see it's not a climate problem, a luck problem or an ABM problem. It's one problem: the plan isn't built on where next year's growth comes from, and the teams aren't working from one version of it. Now they know they have to choose.

**Thinking and feeling:**
- "We can't fund everything. What do we cut?"
- "How do I get sales, marketing and finance to agree on where the growth is?"
- "If I could only bet on three things next year, which three?"
- Feeling: clearer about the problem, anxious about time. The budget date is on the calendar.
- The committee: the CFO wants the gap in dollars. The CEO wants a short list they can defend to the board. The other commercial chair wants to keep what works without funding what doesn't.

**Focus:**
- A few bets that close the gap.
- One version of the truth across sales, marketing and finance.
- Speed. Done before the budget locks.

**What we have:**
- The Ignition Score: ranks segments and accounts by revenue and profitability together.
- Gap math: current, target, and the gap in dollars.
- Straight Shot strategies: ranked segments with owned actions that ladder to revenue.
- From Kate's own client data: only 5% of marketing initiatives were aimed at the most valuable customers, and 90% of tactics weren't aligned to strategy.
- The single source of truth precedent: a management group whose teams spent 15.5 hours per unit on a marketing plan nobody used. Never named.
- "Do less. Make it mean more."

**Impact:** the plan becomes a short list of bets tied to dollars, and the three commercial chairs are arguing from one number.

**Value proposition:** because all three questions come down to one, where next year's growth comes from, we rank your customers and segments by what they're worth and show which few moves close the gap.

**Messages (recommended first):**
- "Three papers. One question: where does next year's growth come from?" Payoff line: "Do less. Make it mean more."
- Alternate, data-led: "5% of the effort aimed at the customers worth the most. That isn't a plan. That's a habit."
- Alternate, cheeky: "Your plan has 27 priorities. Pick three."

## Stage 4: Aware of product (draft)

**What changed:** they've looked at who Kate Burda & Co is and what Ignite does. They want specifics before they spend anyone's time.

**Thinking and feeling:**
- "What does this take from my team in the busiest month of the year?"
- "Is this another platform to roll out, or an answer?"
- "Can I have something before the budget locks?"
- Feeling: interested but protective of the team's time. Allergic to another implementation.
- The committee: the CFO wants cost and what it replaces. IT wants to know what data it touches. The other commercial chair wants to know how many hours it takes from their people.

**Focus:**
- A contained first step with a defined deliverable.
- Fast enough to shape this budget.
- Low lift on a stretched team.
- Not another platform.

**What we have:**
- The Revenue Reveal: senior-led, project-based, delivers the Insight Intelligence Report, with Ignite used inside it.
- Project first. Retainer only once results are running.
- The 90-second Ignite video, the re-cut without the unsourced "19 to 25%" line.

**Account-based note:** this is where the 2 or 3 people at the same company can be invited together: "bring whoever owns the number."

**Impact:** one contained step that sizes the gap and shows where growth comes from, in time for this budget, with no rollout.

**Value proposition:** because you need an answer before the budget locks, not another rollout, we start with the Revenue Reveal: senior-led, one defined deliverable, powered by Ignite, showing what drove last year and where next year's growth comes from.

**Messages (recommended first):**
- "One question, answered before the budget locks." Body leads with: were last year's best results right, or just lucky? That's where the Revenue Reveal starts.
- Alternate: "Not a platform. An answer."
- Account-based alternate: "Bring whoever owns the number."

## Stage 5: Validate (draft)

**What changed:** they're comparing options (the tool sales wants, a big consultancy, their own finance or RevOps team, or us) and getting close to yes. Now they have to defend the choice to the CEO, the CFO, and themselves if it doesn't hold.

**Thinking and feeling:**
- "Why not just have our own team do this?"
- "The big firm has the name. What does a boutique give me?"
- "If I take this to the CEO, will it hold up? What if we're wrong?"
- "Is this a one-time answer, or will we know every quarter?"
- Feeling: skeptical, doing due diligence, and needing cover. Their name is on the number.
- The committee: IT asking if it's another integration. The CFO asking if it's another subscription with unproven return. Sales wants the tool that hands reps names.

**Focus:**
- A clear difference between the options: a filter, not another feed.
- Senior people doing the work, on top of the tools they already have.
- Evidence the answer is right, and lower risk.
- Repeatability, every quarter.

**What we have:**
- Kate's placing line: a sales intelligence tool tells you who to call tomorrow. A revenue growth system tells you where next year's growth comes from and who has to do what to get it.
- Works on top of the CRM and data feeds they already have. Senior-led: Kate does the work, 28 years in.
- The Chesapeake pattern: the tools were used after the stay had happened, to explain the past, not decide the next move. Never named.
- Flying Horse Resort & Club: 11.79% revenue growth year over year, named and willing to be named.
- The 16:1 story: an asset manager overseeing many hotels called a 16 to 1 return a win. Aimed at their least valuable customers, it needed 22 to 1 to break even. The same spend on a more valuable customer cleared the bar at 8 to 1.
- Drive, Coach & Sustain, and Measure & Equip (benchmark scorecard, KPIs). Ignite runs continuously, not once a year.

**Impact:** they see it isn't either-or. They keep their tools, add the judgment that says which data matters, and get results they can explain, repeat and defend.

**Value proposition:** because every option on your list adds more data and your name is on the number, we're the one that tells you which data matters, on top of what you already have, and makes being right something you can prove every quarter.

**Messages (recommended first):**
- "Flying Horse grew 11.79%. Then they kept doing it." Body sets up the filter-not-feed difference, then the proof.
- Alternate: "Your team has the data. Who's telling them what to ignore?"
- Alternate: "Right once is luck. Right every quarter is a system."
- Alternate, story-led: "16:1 looked like a win. It was a loss."
