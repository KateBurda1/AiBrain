**The angle:** ABM contacts are not asking whether to run targeted account campaigns. They are already looking at how to run them well. The angle here skips the "why ABM" conversation entirely and goes straight to the part most ABM efforts get wrong: sales and marketing moving through the same six stages on the same accounts, instead of running two separate plans that happen to target the same company names.

### Entry Sequence

**Step 1, Email, Day 1**
Subject: The ABM problem nobody names

Hi [First Name],

Most account-based marketing programs are not short on target accounts. They are short on sales and marketing moving through the same stages together, on the same accounts, at the same time. When that coordination breaks down, ABM turns into marketing running its own plan and sales running a different one, both aimed at the same logo.

I map that coordination across six stages, from first account signal through expansion, so both teams are working the same account the same way at the same time.

Is that gap, sales and marketing on two different tracks for the same accounts, something [Company] is dealing with?

Warm Regards,
Kate

**Step 2, Email, Day 4**
Subject: Choosing accounts is the easy part

Hi [First Name],

Picking the target account list is the part every ABM program gets right. Building the message, and the internal handoff between marketing and sales, that earns time with the right person inside that account, is where most programs stall.

I call the three pieces that have to move together the Revenue Trifecta: which accounts, what message, and how it ties back to revenue. Miss one and the other two do not hold.

Would it help to compare notes on how [Company]'s ABM motion handles that handoff today?

Warm Regards,
Kate
