## Social and Canva content: the provocation series

### The thesis

**If you're transforming the business and not the commercial team, you're transforming the cost side and hoping the revenue side catches up.**

Most transformations redesign operations, systems and the org chart. Then the same sales and marketing team gets handed the same playbook and is expected to produce new revenue. The commercial team is where strategy meets the customer. If it isn't transformed at the same time, that's where the whole effort quietly stalls. That's the silent saboteur, and it carries a quota.

Kate's lane: revenue strategy to commercial enablement. This series stakes that claim inside the transformation conversation, instead of competing with the Big 4 on "transformation" in general.

**Proof we can use (Kate's own research, HNN article, 30+ commercial leaders, 45+ hours of listening sessions):**
- Teams spent 2 hours a month on reporting and 15 minutes a month on strategy or skill coaching.
- The refrain: "If we want 2019 results, we'll do what we did in 2019."
- More tools and data than ever, and still no revenue lift, because nobody translated the data into how the team sells.

**Format:** Carousel (anchor piece, 5 slides)

**LinkedIn caption:**
Every transformation deck I've seen has a slide for systems, a slide for structure, and a slide for the new operating model.

Rarely one for how the commercial team is going to sell differently on Monday.

So the business gets rebuilt, sales and marketing get a new logo on their email signature, and everyone waits for revenue to follow.

It doesn't. Revenue doesn't transform because the org chart did.

If your commercial team isn't being directed through the transformation at the same time as everyone else, you're not transforming the business. You're transforming the cost side and hoping.

What's on your transformation plan for the people who bring in the revenue?
