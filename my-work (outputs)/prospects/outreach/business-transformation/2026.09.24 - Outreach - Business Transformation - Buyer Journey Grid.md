**Thesis, refined by Kate 2026.09.24:** it's about the top line, not the discipline. Presidents expect a top-line shift from transformation but don't design for it: the revenue goal, the strategies, and the actions that drive it. Kate's framing: **the science and strategy first, then the customer engagement approach.** Revenue should be the plan, not the hoped-for result.

**"Be right, not just lucky":** used where it fits, not forced into every stage. Confirmed by Kate 2026.09.24: **used once only, at Stage 4.** It is the Revenue Reveal's opening question, so the buyer meets the product through it. Not repeated at Stage 5.

**Advisor first, then paid (Kate, 2026.09.24):** Kate finds them, but shows up as an advisor, not a vendor pushing products and services. Stages 1 to 3 give insight and foresight for free. Stage 4 is where it turns into "yes, you should pay me," and the Revenue Reveal is the natural first paid step. Stage 4 keeps "right, or just lucky" plain, with no metaphor, so it lands as the advisor's question.

**How the stages connect:** each value proposition builds on the one before it. The buyer knows more at every stage, so the promise gets more specific: size the shift, then make this quarter pay, then choose the right navigator, then start with one question, then make it hold.

**How each message works:** Stage 1 metaphor (storm becomes climate), Stage 2 metaphor carried forward (the new climate), Stage 3 metaphor (flight plan, navigator), Stage 4 plain advisor's question (right, or just lucky), Stage 5 metaphor closing the thread (fair weather vs the next storm).

**Creative thread:** weather. The shift is a storm that has become the climate. Kate likes satire and metaphors, and tornados, quakes and VUCA as imagery.

## 2026.09.27: 6-stage retrofit in progress (pick up here)

Kate corrected the grid to 6 stages (Point of inspiration, Aware of situation, Informed, Aware of product, Validate, The decision). Under it, **Touch 2 is now Stage 2, Aware of situation**, which this series never had. The old Stage 2 line ("Which part of the plan pays this quarter?") moves to Stage 3, Informed.

**Stage 2 draft (rows accepted, message not found yet):**
- **What changed:** the climate isn't a headline anymore. It's showing up in their own business: a softer quarter, a segment that stopped behaving, a board member asking what the transformation does for revenue.
- **Thinking and feeling:** "This isn't a rough patch. It's the new normal, and it's in our numbers." "We have a transformation plan. Where's revenue in it?" "Are we fixing how we run, or how we grow?" Sobered, a little exposed: the plan is strong on systems and structure, and quiet on the top line. CFO asking when the investment pays back. Sales wondering how the new structure changes how they sell.
- **Focus:** name the real problem before the plan gets more expensive. Find where revenue sits in the transformation. Get ahead of the board's question.
- **What we have:** HNN listening research (30+ leaders, 45 hours, "Nobody said strategy. Nobody said data."), the silent saboteur, "revenue should be the plan," the Built for the Climate report as free insight.
- **Impact:** "something's off" becomes a named gap, revenue not designed into the transformation, seen before the board sees it.
- **Value proposition:** Because the shift is now showing up in your own numbers, we show you where revenue sits in your transformation plan, and where it's missing, before the board asks. Short: Find the revenue gap in your transformation before the board does.

**Message: Kate likes the opener "The weather has changed."** Every second line so far was rejected as not thought-provoking enough: "Your numbers noticed. Did your plan?" / "You've reinforced the roof, the walls and the windows. Who's planning the harvest?" / "Your transformation plan is still dressed for spring." / "So why is revenue still the forecast, and not the plan?" Also offered and not picked: "Your transformation plan has a slide for systems, structure and the operating model. Which slide is revenue?" Start fresh from the thinking-and-feeling row. The line has to make a President stop, not just restate the gap.
