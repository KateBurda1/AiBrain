## 1. What this series has to do

Move a President from "I'm watching this shift" to "I should talk to Kate," over five pieces, without selling until Stage 4. Each piece meets them where their head is at that stage of the journey, and leaves them thinking rather than told.

## 2. Who we're talking to

Pulled live from Zoho, 2026.09.24:
- 324 contacts carrying the Business Transformation intent signal.
- 85% C-suite or President. About two-thirds are CEOs or Presidents, many running a division or region. Only 4 hold a sales or revenue title.
- 72 companies: health systems, financial services, large industrial and consumer brands.
- A third of them are also researching Sales Performance Management or Sales Transformation.

**The person:** a President or CEO who owns the P&L. They answer for the top line, sponsor the transformation, and don't run sales day to day. Senior, busy, allergic to consultants who pitch.

## 3. The insight

Presidents expect transformation to move the top line. They rarely design for it. Systems, structure and operating model get planned in detail. Revenue gets treated as the result they hope follows.

Kate's point of view: **revenue should be the plan.** The science and strategy first (the revenue goal, the strategies, the actions), then the customer engagement approach.

## 4. The strategy: advisor first, then paid

Kate finds them, but shows up as an advisor, not a vendor.
- **Pieces 1 to 3** give insight and foresight. No product, no pitch, no "book a call."
- **Piece 4** is the turn. The Revenue Reveal appears as the natural, contained first step.
- **Piece 5** gives them the proof to say yes, and cover for the board.

## 5. The creative idea: the new climate

The shift they've been watching isn't a storm that passes. It's the new climate. The series follows a leader through it, from the forecast to the flight path to whether their results hold in the next storm.

| # | Stage | The line | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Point of inspiration | "Volatility isn't the weather anymore. It's the climate. Is your revenue plan built for it?" | Size the shift. It's permanent. |
| 2 | Informed | "You've planned for the new climate. Which part of the plan pays this quarter?" | Make the quarterly clock felt. |
| 3 | Explore | "Everyone's got a flight plan. Do you have the right navigator?" | Shift the choice from plans to players. They stay the pilot. |
| 4 | Aware of product | "One question before any big commitment: your best quarter last year, were you right, or just lucky?" | The advisor's question. Opens the Revenue Reveal. |
| 5 | Validate | "Every plan looks good in fair weather. Will your results hold in the next storm?" | Proof that it lasts. |

**Rules the lines follow:**
- Every line traces back to what the buyer is thinking and feeling, what they care about, and what we have at that stage. Clever but untethered is cut.
- "Right, or just lucky" appears once, in piece 4. Never repeated.
- The buyer is always the pilot. Nothing implies we take the controls or replace their people.

## 6. Tone

Clever, provocative, a little sarcastic. Satire and metaphor over explanation. Short lines that build, then land. Warm underneath: an advisor who has seen this before, not a critic.

Taken from Kate's own work (the "ABM It just doesn't make sense" piece):
- **Suspense through pacing.** "Have you ever looked at something you've seen over and over again. And all of a sudden..." The ellipsis does the work.
- **A reveal in pink capitals.** "COMPLETELY DIFFERENT THAN YOU EVER HAD BEFORE?"
- **Permission to be irreverent.** "this *^%& doesn't make sense anymore." "Psst- Don't fall for the pyramid thingy... you've outgrown it."
- **A turn toward better.** "There is a better way."

Never: em dashes, the word "actually," jargon, anything that reads as AI.

## 7. Format: carousels with movement

Kate likes carousels and movement, like a short video that can be embedded. Each piece is built as a **motion carousel**: a 20 to 30 second text-led animation, the same length and rhythm as the ABM piece.

| Deliverable | Spec | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Motion piece (MP4) | 1080 x 1350, 20 to 30 seconds, no sound needed, captions built in | LinkedIn native video (autoplays in feed), website embed |
| Animated GIF | Same, compressed to under 5 MB | Email (video doesn't play in most inboxes, GIFs do), Zoho cadence steps |
| Carousel PDF | Same frames as still slides | LinkedIn document post, for anyone who prefers to swipe |
| Cover still (PNG) | First or strongest frame | Email thumbnail linking to the video, Zoho, website |

## 8. Look and feel

Based on Kate's ABM motion piece, which is the closest thing to how she wants this to feel:

- **Type:** an elegant, high-contrast serif, centered. The ABM piece uses a Playfair-style serif. The brand serif is Freight Big, with Playfair Display (free) as the stand-in where Freight isn't installed.
- **Backgrounds:** frames alternate between clean white and deep black. White for the setup, black for the turn and the reveal.
- **Color:** Kate Burda pink (PMS 214, #D51067) for the reveal line only, set in capitals. Everything else black on white or white on black.
- **One striking image per piece, black and white.** The ABM piece used an eye peering through torn paper. This series: a tornado for piece 1, cockpit or instrument panel for piece 3, clearing sky for piece 5. Stock or AI-generated, always black and white.
- **Divider and logo:** a short thin rule, then the KB Kate Burda & Co. horizontal logo in pink, centered at the bottom of each frame.
- **Movement:** text fades or types in line by line, holds, then cuts. Piece 1's tornado is built from the four words (VOLATILE, UNCERTAIN, COMPLEX, AMBIGUOUS) spinning into a funnel.

**Note on the earlier visuals:** the first set of slides (bold sans-serif, black and hot pink, "COMMERCIAL EXCELLENCE" eyebrow) follows the Canva Commercial Excellence series. It doesn't match the ABM motion style, and its pink (#d53d72) isn't the brand pink (#D51067). Those get rebuilt to this brief.
