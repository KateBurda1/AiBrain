A five-minute check on the spaces in between

How long from signal to result?

Someone spotted it. Maybe someone heard it. Months later, you're looking for any shred of evidence it worked. Six questions to find out how long yours takes.

Your team can see the market better than ever. Intent signals, in-market lists, who to call next. That's real, and it works.

Now follow one signal all the way through. Someone spots it. It has to be heard by someone who can act. It has to become a plan, the team has to get around it, and then the work has to happen. Then you wait for a result.

Each handoff is a place where time leaks.

Nobody owns the spaces in between. Data is only as good as execution. The winner isn't who sees it first. It's who gets there first.

The check

Six questions. Time each gap.

Think of the last shift your team spotted. How long did each step take?

Add it up.

Answer the first five to see your number.

Each answer adds time. Days count as about half a week, weeks as about three, and months as about three months.

What it looks like

The lag is real.

In companies we've worked with, a trends person finds the shift early. Whether it's heard depends on the relationship. Then it goes like this.

  • It takes a long time to settle.
  • Longer to get the team around it.
  • Then the work.

Months later, everyone is looking for any shred of evidence it paid off.

Arrive months later, and you arrive when everyone else does.

You don't have to take our word for it

What the thinkers say

Mankins and Steele, Harvard Business Review

Most companies' strategies deliver only 63% of their promised financial value. Strategy loses a third of its worth on the way to results.

McKinsey, 2019

Only 20 percent of leaders say their organizations excel at decision making, and faster decisions tend to be higher quality. Speed doesn't cost you quality.

John Boyd, military strategist

Winning means getting inside the other side's decision cycle. The one who acts first sets the pace.

The difference

What a plan that closes the gaps does

  • It ranks segments and accounts by revenue and profitability together, so "what gives the best results" stops being an opinion.
  • It turns that into one plan with owners and dollars that every team and region can run.
  • It makes room by stopping low-return activity, so nothing gets stacked on a full team.
  • It makes change land in steps sized to the capacity you have.
