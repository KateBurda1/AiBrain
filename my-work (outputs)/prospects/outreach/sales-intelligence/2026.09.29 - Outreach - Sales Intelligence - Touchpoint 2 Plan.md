## The message

**Headline:** Your tools know who to call. Your team knows how to win. Does your plan know which customers are worth winning?

**Why it's true.** Most leadership teams have invested in knowing their market: contacts, intent signals, lists of who's in-market right now. The team took it and ran. Activity is up. But next year's plan still starts from last year's number plus a percentage. Sales intelligence answers who to call tomorrow. It doesn't answer which customers drive profitable growth, and those aren't the same list. Nobody's doing anything wrong. The plan just never asked the question.

### Draft copy

**Worth Winning?**
*A five-minute check before you lock next year's plan.*

You've invested in knowing your market. Your team knows who's in-market, who to call and how to win them. That's real, and it's working.

Now look at next year's plan. Where does the growth come from? For most teams, it's last year's number plus a percentage, spread across the same segments as always.

Your sales intelligence tells your team who to call tomorrow. It doesn't tell you which customers drive profitable growth. Those aren't the same list, and the plan is where the difference shows up.

**Six questions. Answer yes or no.**

1. Can you name the customers or segments that drive most of your profit, not just your revenue?
2. Are they ranked by revenue and profitability together, or by size?
3. Would your head of sales, your CMO and your CFO name the same list?
4. Does next year's growth number say which customers it comes from?
5. Is your market data used to decide where to aim before the plan locks, or to explain results after?
6. Is your best return aimed at your most valuable customers, or your easiest ones?

**Mostly yes:** you know who's worth winning. Keep testing it.
**Mostly no:** your tools are working harder than your plan. You're not alone, and it's fixable.

**What we see in budget reviews**

In our own data, only 5% of initiatives were aimed at the most valuable customers, and 90% of tactics weren't aligned to the overall strategy. That's not a talent problem. Teams are working hard. It's a planning problem: nobody told them which customers were worth the effort.

**Right number, wrong customer**

An asset manager overseeing many hotels reported a 16 to 1 return on ad spend. Against the customers it was aimed at, it needed 22 to 1 to break even, so the win was a loss. The same spend aimed at a more valuable customer would have cleared the bar at 8 to 1. Every number was correct. It was measured against the wrong customer.

**What the thinkers say** *(verified 2026.09.29)*

- **Peter Drucker:** "There is surely nothing quite so useless as doing with great efficiency what should not be done at all."
- **Robert Kaplan and V.G. Narayanan** found the most profitable 20% of customers typically generate 150% to 300% of total profits, while the least profitable 10% to 20% lose 50% to 200%. Revenue and profit rank customers differently.

**What a plan built on the right customers does**

- It ranks segments and accounts by revenue and profitability together, so "our most valuable customers" stops being an opinion.
- It starts from what those customers are trying to achieve, not only who is showing intent.
- It points the team's effort and the budget at the customers worth winning.
- It gives every number in the plan a reason behind it.

**Want the full paper?** Right, or Just Lucky? 10 questions to ask before you lock next year's budget. (link to the report form)

**Want to talk it through?** 20 minutes, no pitch. (booking link)
