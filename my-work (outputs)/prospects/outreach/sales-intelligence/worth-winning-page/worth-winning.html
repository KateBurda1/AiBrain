A five-minute check before you lock next year's plan

Worth winning?

Your tools know who to call. Your team knows how to win. Does your plan know which customers are worth winning? Six questions to find out.

You've invested in knowing your market. Your team knows who's in-market, who to call and how to win them. That's real, and it's working.

Now look at next year's plan. Where does the growth come from? For most teams, it's last year's number plus a percentage, spread across the same segments as always.

Your sales intelligence tells your team who to call tomorrow.

It doesn't tell you which customers drive profitable growth. Those aren't the same list, and the plan is where the difference shows up.

The check

Six questions. Answer yes or no.

1

Can you name the customers or segments that drive most of your profit, not just your revenue?

2

Are they ranked by revenue and profitability together, or by size?

3

Would your head of sales, your CMO and your CFO name the same list?

4

Does next year's growth number say which customers it comes from?

5

Is your market data used to decide where to aim before the plan locks, not only to explain results after?

6

Is your best return aimed at your most valuable customers, not just your easiest ones?

Mostly yes: you know who's worth winning.

Keep testing it. Mostly no: your tools are working harder than your plan. You're not alone, and it's fixable.

Right number, wrong customer

A 16 to 1 return, and it was a loss.

16:1Reported return
22:1Needed to break even
8:1Enough, aimed right

An asset manager overseeing many hotels reported a 16 to 1 return on ad spend and called it a win. The campaign was aimed at their least valuable customers. Against that segment, it needed 22 to 1 just to break even.

The same spend aimed at a more valuable customer would have cleared the bar at 8 to 1. Every number was correct. It was measured against the wrong customer.

A lower return would have been the better outcome.

You don't have to take our word for it

What the thinkers say

Peter Drucker

"There is surely nothing quite so useless as doing with great efficiency what should not be done at all."

Robert Kaplan and V.G. Narayanan, Harvard Business School

Their research on customer profitability found the most profitable 20% of customers typically generate 150% to 300% of total profits, while the least profitable 10% to 20% lose 50% to 200%. Revenue and profit rank customers differently.

The difference

What a plan built on the right customers does

  • It ranks segments and accounts by revenue and profitability together, so "our most valuable customers" stops being an opinion.
  • It starts from what those customers are trying to achieve, not only who is showing intent.
  • It points the team's effort and the budget at the customers worth winning.
  • It gives every number in the plan a reason behind it.
