## Descriptor collision, do not harmonize these

**Both unnamed clients are New Orleans companies.** If they carry similar descriptors, a reader will merge them into one story or start triangulating. Keep them deliberately different and keep them apart.

| Case | Descriptor used externally | Never say |
|---|---|---|
| 16:1 ROAS | "a hotel in a major leisure market" | anything narrower |
| 15.5 hours | "a hotel management group" | any city, market, or region |

The 15.5-hour client's offices are in New Orleans but its hotels are in other markets entirely, so any geographic descriptor would be both inaccurate and identifying. Do not add one, and do not "tidy" these two descriptors into a matching pair. They look mismatched on purpose.

**Related risk:** hold the 16:1 description at exactly its current level of specificity. "A hotel in a major leisure market" is vague enough. Add the market, the segment, or the campaign type and a well-connected operator could start guessing.

## Weak points in the current draft, flagged honestly

- **The strongest asset in the document is unattributed and permanently will be.** That is the right call, but the gap between the 16:1 story's persuasive weight and its verifiability is wide. A principal who is moved by it will ask which client. Kate needs a comfortable, practiced answer for that moment, and it should sound like discretion rather than evasion. Something close to: "I don't name clients who missed. That's why you'd work with me."
- **The proven/unproven split is honest, and honesty has a cost.** Saying plainly that realized lift is unproven is the most differentiated thing in the document, and it also hands a cautious asset manager a clean reason to wait for the beta's results rather than fund them. The counterweight is first-mover scarcity, which currently rests on one sentence about patterns going into the engine first. **The strongest available lever is a concrete post-beta pricing advantage for founding partners, which is open item 1.** Deciding that TBD would materially strengthen the close.
- **Page one is dense.** Two stories, a proof ledger, terms, and a CTA. It works because the 16:1 panel gives the eye somewhere to land, but layout has to respect that hierarchy or the page reads as a wall.
- **The asset-manager audience is not served by this document.** Per the positioning doc they are influencers, not purchasers, and their pitch is "a way to know whether what you are being told is true," not "a better tool." This one-pager aims at the economic buyer. It will get forwarded to asset managers and land at maybe 60%. A second, shorter cut is worth doing before the Crescent conversation specifically.

## Positioning guardrails carried into this document

- **Category:** Revenue Growth System for multi-property portfolios. Never "commercial operating system," never bare "revenue," never anything that reads as a revenue management system.
- **The placing line goes early**, before any argument: revenue management decides what to charge tonight, the Revenue Growth System decides where next year's growth comes from and who has to do what to get it.
- **"Were we right, or just lucky?" is the product thesis, not a tagline.** It leads the proof section.
- **Never imply single-task causation at a single property.** Correlation of patterns across a portfolio is the claim. Single-task causation is what breaks under diligence.
- **Alignment is the mechanism, recovered revenue is the benefit.** Never pitch alignment as the benefit itself.
- **Fuse is not built.** Described as being built alongside beta partners' real portfolios, and framed as the reason a partner commits multiple assets rather than as an apology.
- **Tenure:** 28+ years to Kate personally, "the decade since 2016" to the firm. Never 28 years to the company.
- **Style:** no em dashes, no "actually," American spelling, short sentences, nothing that reads as AI-generated.
