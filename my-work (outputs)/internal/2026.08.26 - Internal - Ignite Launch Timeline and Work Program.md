## 1. What this launch is

This is a company launch, not a beta rollout with a short prospect list attached to it. Ignite is a new line of business Kate Burda & Company is bringing to market, and it needs the same rigor as launching a company, because that's what it is. The core strategy: Thoughtware engagements are the proof-of-value wedge that converts well-capitalized, multi-property hotel owners into Ignite's first paying customers, using real consulting relationships to build and validate the product instead of guessing at features in a vacuum. The near-term target is 3 to 10 strategic partners, cash-for-access at $15,000 to $25,000 each, each committing multiple properties to the beta. Finn's analysis (in the source plan) recommends narrowing that to 3 to 5 given the team's actual capacity; that recommendation is not yet a final decision, and is flagged again in section 5.

## 3. Sequencing logic

- **Positioning has to lock before pitch materials get built.** Dunford's method is explicit that messaging written before positioning is settled has to be rewritten once positioning changes. Running it out of order wastes the pitch-writing work in Phase 1.
- **The team-credentials fix and pricing update have to happen before any pitch material goes to a prospect**, not after. The Makeready template currently misrepresents the team (lists an inactive person) and carries three retired pricing structures. Sending it as-is would be a factual accuracy problem, not a style issue.
- **The Fuse framing decision has to be made before the pitch is finalized**, because the pitch cannot describe a capability that doesn't exist yet without saying so. This is a precondition for the pitch materials task, not a parallel task.
- **The financial model rebuild informs pricing and runway confidence**, so it should be substantially done before Phase 4 (beta execution) begins committing partners, even though it can run in parallel with Phase 1's pitch work. Without it, nobody knows if $15,000-$25,000 times 3 to 10 partners funds what the beta needs to deliver.
- **A written pilot agreement has to exist before outreach starts**, not be drafted in parallel with early conversations. Per Scout's research, this is the most common failure point in enterprise pilots, and institutional buyers like Noble or Crescent will expect terms in hand before they commit budget, not after.
- **A stated data-security posture has to exist before or during outreach**, for the same reason: procurement and IT review at this tier happens before money moves, not after a verbal yes.
- **CRM discipline has to be working before outreach scales**, or the pipeline gap that exists today (zero logged deals) just reappears the moment real conversations start.
- **The team-capacity plan has to exist before the onboarding wave sequence is decided.** You cannot sequence how many partners to bring on at once without first knowing how many properties the 4-person team can support while Fuse is still being built.
- **Beta execution cannot start until Phase 2 (legal and ops readiness) is functionally complete.** Onboarding a partner without a signed agreement or a stated security posture recreates exactly the gaps Scout flagged.
- **The expansion/fundraising decision only makes sense after the beta produces real usage data.** It is a Phase 5 decision by design, not something to pre-decide now.

## 4. Capacity flag

The Ignite team is four people: Kate, Eddie Westerfield (CTO), Recardo Poole (UI/UX), and Tristan Carty (Operations). None of them work on Ignite alone. Kate is also running Thoughtware and this entire strategy process, Eddie is the only person building Fuse, Recardo is the only person on interface work for both the admin product and the eventual multi-property configuration layer, and Tristan is the only operations person, which likely means Tristan becomes the default point of contact for every onboarded partner unless that's explicitly assigned otherwise.

This is a real constraint on the timeline above, not a detail to smooth over. Specifically:

- **The team cannot onboard 10 partners at once while also building Fuse and running Thoughtware engagements.** Finn's read in the source plan is that 3 to 5 well-resourced partnerships, each committing a few properties, can produce 10 to 20-plus properties of real data, likely enough to prove the Ignition Score and multi-property capability. Ten thin relationships run by four people risks worse data, not better traction.
- **Fuse is being built during the beta, not before it.** That means Eddie's time is split between shipping the capability partners are paying to help build and whatever else CTO work requires day to day. A capacity plan (Phase 2) needs to say plainly how much of Eddie's time Fuse gets versus everything else.
- **Support load scales with properties, not partners.** A partner committing five properties creates roughly five times the support and configuration work of a partner committing one, even though it counts as a single "partner" in the 3-to-10 target.
- **There is no dedicated onboarding, support, or customer-success role.** Whichever of the four people ends up doing partner support is doing it on top of their existing job, not instead of it.

None of this means the beta shouldn't happen. It means the wave sequencing and partner count in section 5 need to be decided with this ceiling in view, not decided first and squared with capacity later.
