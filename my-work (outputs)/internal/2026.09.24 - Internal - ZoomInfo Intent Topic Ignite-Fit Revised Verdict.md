## What was wrong with the first pass

1. **I assumed account-level scoping that doesn't exist.** Kate: "the intents are contact based not company based." There is no target-account restriction on these 12 topics today — any contact at any company can trip a topic and enroll, subject only to whatever company filters sit on the underlying ZoomInfo workflow (currently none that scope to hospitality — see below). So the honest frame isn't "right company, wrong person." It's closer to "any company, any person," which means topic-name plausibility alone was doing too much work in my first verdict.

2. **I graded topics against a made-up ICP instead of the real one.** Ignite is in **beta, not GA**, with 5 named target partners (Noble Investment Group — Stephen Nicholas, Ben Brunt; Raines — Kerry Ranson; Crescent Hotels & Resorts; PM Hotel Group; plus backups: National Hospitality Services, Midas Hospitality, Hawkeye Hotels, Valencia Group, Sandpiper Hospitality), all multi-property hotel owner/operator groups. I dropped Sales Intelligence on "sales-ops employee shopping for tools" pattern-matching instead of checking it against what Ignite's beta partners are actually evaluating. Kate's pushback: "the sales intelligence is important to ignite's customers as we look through beta... you should be looking at some of that strategy so we are getting optimized vs. just activity output."

## The bigger finding: the topic list isn't the lever, the company filter is

I checked what companies are actually showing up under these 12 topics today (`2026.08.26 - Internal - Zoho Sales Transformation Intent Cadence Spec.md`, the real pull). Across all 12, the named companies are Pfizer, NVIDIA, Snowflake, Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, Delta, JetBlue, UPS, Cisco, ServiceNow, Verizon, Estee Lauder, Aramark, and similar — **zero hospitality or multi-property portfolio names show up in any topic's example list, including the ones I kept.** That's because this workflow was built and filtered for a different engagement (`2026.08.26 - Internal - Zoho Intent Branching Engagement Spec.md` says explicitly: "Scope: Thoughtware only. Nothing here touches Ignite, hospitality, or the beta partner program.")

So the real problem isn't which 8 or 12 topic names sound Ignite-relevant. **It's that the existing contact pool, whichever topics you pick from it, was never filtered to Ignite-relevant companies in the first place.** Picking "the right 8 topic names" out of a pool that's structurally Pfizer-and-NVIDIA-shaped doesn't fix that.

**The fix already has precedent.** The ABM Intent Targeting and Intent Signal Targeting ZoomInfo workflows were already rebuilt (9/19) with Company Filters — Revenue $1B+, Public/Private, US/Company HQ — sitting alongside the topic/intent trigger. That proves ZoomInfo's workflow builder supports a firmographic company filter independent of which intent topic(s) it's paired with. The same mechanism can carry an **Industry = Hotels/Hospitality** (or equivalent NAICS/SIC hospitality code) filter, layered onto any topic or combination of topics, without naming the 5 specific target accounts. That gets you "real Ignite-relevant companies" the way Kate asked, without collapsing into strict ABM account-list targeting.

**Recommendation: build this as a separate ZoomInfo workflow (new, hospitality-filtered) rather than reusing the existing Thoughtware-scoped one.** Reusing the current pool for Ignite messaging means sending Ignite pitches to people who tripped a topic while working at Pfizer or NVIDIA — wrong audience regardless of topic choice, and it blurs a spec that was deliberately scoped away from Ignite. A parallel workflow — same 12 topics, new hospitality company filter, new Ignite-flavored copy — is the clean way to do this.

## Topic-by-topic, corrected

**Sales Intelligence — reinstate, high priority once filtered.**
Kate's read is right and mine wasn't grounded: a commercial/revenue exec at a multi-property hospitality group evaluating sales intelligence tooling as part of a broader revenue-tech stack review (CRM enrichment, account/deal scoring, pipeline visibility) is a real, common motion for operators at Ignite's scale — and it's adjacent to what Ignite's own Sales/ABM engine already does. This isn't "sales-ops person shopping for tools" pattern-matching; it's "commercial leader auditing their stack," which is exactly Ignite's pitch (do less, make it mean more, one system instead of three). Small pool today (37, paired with Revenue Intelligence) — that's fine, precision over volume matters more here than reach.

**Sales Performance Management — reinstate, high-medium priority.**
This maps directly onto a real Ignite product surface: **Ignite Talent** (competency assessment, manager/team gap analysis, coaching) and the "Drive, Coach & Sustain" methodology step. A hospitality commercial or HR leader evaluating sales performance management — quota design, coaching cadence, comp — is close to a literal match for what that module solves, not just a loose topical connection. Stronger case than Sales Intelligence on product fit, weaker only because the pool (262) is more likely to be non-hospitality noise until filtered.

**B2B Marketing — reinstate, but lower priority, and check filtered volume before building it.**
Plausible fit exists (group sales / corporate marketing arms of hotel management companies market to meeting planners, developers, corporate accounts — a real B2B-marketing motion inside hospitality), but it's a narrower, more indirect match than the two above. Don't build a full 5-touch Ignite-flavored track on this one until the hospitality filter is live and you can see whether it actually returns meaningful volume. If it returns a handful of contacts, fold it into a combined track rather than building it standalone.

**Digital Marketing — leave dropped, or dead last if built at all.**
Still the weakest signal, filter or no filter. It's the largest, most generic pool (693–711) precisely because "runs digital marketing" is close to a universal hygiene topic — almost every company of any size is doing *something* here, so an intent tag on this topic carries the least specific information about buying intent even inside a hospitality-filtered pool. If you build it at all, build it last and expect it to add volume more than precision.
