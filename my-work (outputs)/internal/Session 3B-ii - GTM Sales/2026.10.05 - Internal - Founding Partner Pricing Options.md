## 1. Beta price recommendation

**Recommend $20,000 per partner, one flat price, for up to 5 committed properties over the 6-month beta.**

| Price per partner | Total at 5 partners | Above the $23,500 known need | Partners needed to cover the need |
|---|---|---|---|
| $4,700 (cost solve) | $23,500 | $0 | All 5 |
| $15,000 (band floor) | $75,000 | $51,500 | 2 |
| **$20,000 (recommended)** | **$100,000** | **$76,500** | **2** |
| $25,000 (band ceiling) | $125,000 | $101,500 | 1 |

Why $20,000:

- **$4,700 is a cost floor, not a price.** It covers conferences, travel and legal. It does not cover any development, any salary, or Kate's time working alongside each partner's commercial team, which is the most valuable thing the beta offers. Pricing at cost tells a multi-property group this is a science project.
- **It covers the known need with only two partners signed.** That matters, because five signatures this year is a goal, not a fact.
- **The $76,500 above the known need is the reserve for what is still unpriced:** Eddie's development and infrastructure lines, a legal quote that is still an estimate, and a possible refund if Fuse slips (one of the pilot agreement terms still to be drafted). Until that refund window closes, only treat the known $23,500 as safe to spend.
- **One number, not a band.** Quoting $15,000 to $25,000 invites every buyer to negotiate to $15,000. Quote $20,000 and hold it.
- **It is roughly what 6 months of Ignite would cost at the 2024 list anyway** ($8,100 subscription plus $11,050 onboarding is $19,150). So the beta is not discounted software. It is software plus Kate's team plus build input, which is exactly how the one-pager positions it.

One thing to decide alongside this: Noble, Raines, Crescent and PM Hotel Group are on both the Ignite target list and Session 1's $25,000 Advisory target list. Decide whether a dual-purpose account pays both, or gets a bundle, before anyone has that conversation.

## 2. Three founding-partner options

All three assume the same base case. A partner with 5 properties, 2024 list of $16,200 a year, three years after the beta ($48,600 a partner at list, $243,000 for all five), and every partner converts. Onboarding is waived in all three because beta partners are already onboarded, so it costs Ignite little. "L" below means one year of GA list for that partner.

### Option 1: Rate lock

**What the partner gets.** Whatever the GA list price is at launch, theirs does not move for three years. Onboarding waived on any property they add.

**What it costs Ignite over 3 years.** Only the price increases Ignite gives up. The files have no planned increase, so this is shown two ways:

| Annual list increase (assumption) | Cost per partner (3 years) | Cost for 5 partners | As % of L |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | $0 | $0 | 0% |
| 5% | $2,471 | $12,353 | 15% of one year |
| 10% | $5,022 | $25,110 | 31% of one year |

**Risk.** Low cost, low pull. Locking a price nobody has seen yet is hard to value from the buyer's side. It also works against Ignite if the launch price turns out too low, because then it is locked for three years.

**How it reads in a sales conversation.** "Whatever we launch at, your price does not move for three years." Reasonable, but nobody signs for it.

### Option 2: Founding discount (recommended)

**What the partner gets.** 20% off GA list for three years, on every property they bring on, not just the ones in the beta. Onboarding waived. Does not stack with the multi-year discount in the 2024 rate sheet (that sheet already offers 20% for a multi-year commitment, so the founding rate replaces it).

**What it costs Ignite over 3 years.** 20% of list, scaling with what the partner buys.

| | Per partner | For 5 partners |
|---|---|---|
| At 5 properties ($16,200 a year list) | $9,720 | $48,600 |
| Per property, per year | $600 | |
| As % of list | 20% (0.6 x L over three years) | |

**Risk.** It is a real margin give, and a discount is hard to take back once given. That is why it has a hard three-year end and a clear "founding" label. The upside of the structure: the cost grows only when revenue grows, so it never takes cash Ignite does not already have coming in.

**How it reads in a sales conversation.** "Founding partners pay 20% under list for three years, on every property you bring on, not just the ones in the beta." Clear, easy to repeat to a CFO, and it rewards rolling Ignite out across the portfolio.

### Option 3: Beta fee credit

**What the partner gets.** Half the beta fee ($10,000 of $20,000) credited toward GA year one. Onboarding waived.

**What it costs Ignite over 3 years.** $10,000 per partner, $50,000 for all five, about 21% of three years of list. All of it lands in GA year one, where a partner's revenue drops from $16,200 to $6,200 (down 62%).

A 100% credit is off the table. At the 2024 list, $20,000 is more than a full year of subscription, so year one would be free with $3,800 left over, and the cost would equal every dollar the beta brought in ($100,000 across five).

**Risk.** Highest of the three. It turns beta cash into a prepayment Ignite owes back in product. It hits year one, when Ignite needs cash most. And it undercuts the pitch: the one-pager says partners are "generating the proof," not buying software at a discount. A credit says the beta fee was a down payment on software.

**How it reads in a sales conversation.** "Half of your beta fee comes back to you as credit against year one." It lowers the perceived risk of saying yes, which is real, but it trains the buyer to see the beta as a deposit.

### Side by side

| | Rate lock | Founding discount | Beta fee credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Partner gets | Price frozen 3 years | 20% off list, 3 years, every property | $10,000 credit in GA year one |
| 3-year cost, 5 partners | $0 to $25,110 | $48,600 | $50,000 |
| When the cost hits | Years 2 and 3 | Spread evenly, scales with revenue | All in year one |
| Pull in the room | Weak | Strong | Medium |
| Main risk | Locks in an untested price | Margin given away | Undercuts the "you're paying for the proof" story |

## 3. Recommendation

Price the beta at $20,000 flat and make the founding advantage 20% off GA list for three years, on every property the partner brings on, with onboarding waived and no stacking with other discounts. The founding discount is the only option that pays the partner more the further they roll Ignite across their portfolio, and cross-portfolio rollout is the exact proof Ignite does not have yet: the one-pager says plainly that Flying Horse cannot show a cross-portfolio pattern. So the incentive and the business need point the same way. Its cost, about $48,600 across five partners over three years at the 2024 list, only shows up as revenue comes in, so it never competes with the beta cash. The rate lock is cheaper but asks a buyer to value a price they have not seen, and the beta credit turns the beta into a deposit and spends the cash in Ignite's leanest year. One condition: say "20% under list" in the room this week if needed, but set the real GA list in Session 4 before anything goes in a signed agreement, because 20% off an unknown number is a promise, not a price.

## 4. Per-partner success metrics, starter list

Pricing and proof go together in the pitch. Agree these in writing with each partner before day one, in their numbers. Targets are set with each partner, not here, because inventing them would be the exact thing Ignite says it does not do.

| # | Metric | How it is measured | Baseline | When |

| 1 | **Revenue identified** | Dollars of overlooked revenue Ignite surfaces per committed property, ranked by Ignition Score | The partner's current commercial plan for each property | By month 2 |

| 4 | **Planning hours and plan use** | Above-property hours to produce a commercial plan, and the share of the plan's ranked actions completed | The partner's own hours, with 15.5 hours per hotel as the reference point | Month 1 and month 6 |

Metric 5 is the one the founding discount pays for. If a partner brings on more properties, there are more chances to prove it.
